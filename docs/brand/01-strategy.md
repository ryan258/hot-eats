# Brand strategy and positioning

## Brand platform

| Decision | Working direction |
| --- | --- |
| Name | Hot Eats |
| First-use descriptor | Restaurant website kit |
| Category | Configurable restaurant websites |
| Audience | Independent restaurants and the web partners who build for them |
| Promise | Give guests the information they need to choose a visit and a clear next step. |
| Campaign line | Make it easy to come in. |
| Purpose | Make the restaurant’s own website useful on an ordinary service day. |
| Personality | Welcoming, direct, observant, and dependable |
| Operating idea | Give the restaurant one structured place to maintain the guest facts its site displays. |

**Positioning statement.** For independent restaurants with menus, schedules, and service options that change, Hot Eats is a configurable website kit that puts the restaurant’s identity and the details guests need on the same site. It gives a web partner a reusable build and gives the restaurant an explicit way to review and update key facts. The site can hand guests off to the restaurant’s existing ordering or booking providers when destinations are configured.

**Product story.** A guest checks the menu on the way out the door. Someone else needs to know which location serves lunch, whether the patio has a step, or where pickup happens. The answers should be easy to read and specific to the restaurant. Hot Eats starts from those questions and gives each restaurant room for its own menu, photography, and personality.

## Audience and buying moment

| Audience | Trigger and job | What to show | Common friction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Independent owner or GM | A dated menu, holiday-hours confusion, a remodel, a new service, or a site refresh | How to confirm today's hours, share a dish, and pause one service | Time to gather facts, update habits, and approve the site |
| Restaurant web designer or small studio | Multiple clients need familiar restaurant features and distinct visual treatments | A client copy, a preset comparison, a component example, and the local release workflow | Stack fit, handoff boundaries, maintenance, and migration effort |
| Small multi-location operator | Guests need the right menu, hours, or service for each location | Location selection, relevant menus and actions, and route-specific schedules | Reliable per-location facts and a named owner at each location |
| Guest | Decide where to go and what to do now | Readable menu, visit facts, and a specific action | An incorrect location, a stale time, an unavailable service, or a broken handoff |

Begin with a single-location restaurant or café that already has a web operator, a current menu, and one visibly frustrating guest task. Use the food truck and multi-location designs to show the kit’s range. Expand the offer after the team has rehearsed regular updates with a real operator.

A client seeking a self-serve editor, integrated commerce, or managed support needs an explicit delivery and integration discussion. The current kit uses repository-based configuration and local tools. The publisher and provider account owner need to be named in the engagement.

## Message pillars and proof

| Pillar | What the audience should understand | What can be demonstrated today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guest decisions come first | Menus, hours, locations and visit details deserve prominent, specific pages. | Browse and filter a text menu, follow a dish link, and compare a café, a truck schedule and a multi-location visit page. |
| The restaurant has its own identity | The restaurant’s story and visual style can carry through its site. | Compare different preset treatments and use the same component in distinct restaurant designs. |
| Changes have a workflow | Someone can review what changed before it reaches the published site. | Show a local preview diff, a menu edit and a recovery snapshot in a disposable demo copy. |
| Existing service choices carry through | The website can guide a guest to an established service destination. | Show an illustrative unavailable or paused state. Walk through an approved destination only after the restaurant provides and checks it. |

## Brand architecture

- **Hot Eats** is the product and partner-facing identity. Use it on product explanations, sales collateral, product demos, and documentation.
- **Lucyd** is the illustrative diner. Label it as a fictional example in product and sales materials. Treat other sample brands, menus, dates, and reviews the same way.
- **A client restaurant** leads on its guest site, menu, print pieces, provider screens, and contact details. Any product credit requires agreement and has no bearing on the client’s visual system.
- **Presets and components** describe selectable styles and functions. Use a restaurant type or guest task in sales copy. Keep preset IDs and template names in technical materials.

The name can sound like a dining guide or food business on its own. Keep the descriptor beside it on first exposure, in profile bios and in press metadata. See [the market and naming evidence](06-market-and-swot.md) before public rollout.

## Offer architecture to validate

Use the pilot to learn which of these scopes buyers can actually operate.

| Proposed scope | What the engagement would cover | What must be agreed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restaurant website pilot | Guest-task review, one preset adapted to one restaurant, verified core content, one routine update rehearsal, and a local release review | Access to facts and assets, accountable reviewer, change process, hosting and publication roles |
| Location and service expansion | Additional location pages, location-specific menus, service states, and approved handoffs | Who confirms each location and who checks each provider |
| Web partner delivery | Reusable kit, component reference, adaptation notes, and a client handoff | Rights, support, update ownership, hosting, and incident response |

The pilot scope is an offer hypothesis. The repository establishes the build capabilities. Pricing, terms, staffing, service levels, public availability, and owner acceptance need decisions before an offer goes live.
