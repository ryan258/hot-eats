# Voice, messaging, and claims

## Core copy

| Placement | Copy |
| --- | --- |
| Product name and descriptor | Hot Eats · Restaurant website kit |
| Campaign headline | Make it easy to come in. |
| One sentence | Hot Eats is a configurable website kit for independent restaurants and the web partners who build for them. |
| Landing-page deck | Give guests a clear menu, the right hours and location, and a straightforward next step on a site that feels like your restaurant. |

| Demo call to action | Explore restaurant examples |

Use the pilot call to action once there is an approved contact destination. Pair the campaign headline with the product descriptor where a reader might think Hot Eats is a restaurant.

## Voice rules

- **Welcoming.** Write like a capable host. Use “your restaurant,” “guests,” and concrete situations. “Help guests find the right location” is enough.
- **Direct.** Put the task in the first sentence. Lead a link with a verb or a destination, such as “Check today’s hours,” “Get directions,” or “Order pickup.”
- **Specific.** Say what a workflow actually does. “Preview a menu change and save a recovery snapshot” gives a partner something to verify.
- **Dependable.** State who takes the next step and what happens there. “Continue to [Provider] to reserve a table” sets the expectation before the link.
- **Calm when service changes.** Lead with the state and an actionable fallback. Use a date and time zone when the message crosses locations or can be shared out of context.

Write in sentence case. Use everyday nouns and short, active sentences. Spell the product **Hot Eats** and the diner example **Lucyd**. Use “website kit” with buyers, “preset” for a selectable starting design, and “Hugo component” in implementation contexts. Use “website” for the restaurant’s public site and “provider” for the service that completes the action.

## Claims guide

| Safe claim for current product materials | Evidence to show or keep with the claim | Evidence needed for a stronger claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text menus can include filters, sold-out labels and printable views. | Use the actual menu page and applicable presets. | Real-device and physical print checks for a specific restaurant. |
| The kit supports location pages, schedule exceptions and reviewed access details. | Show the appropriate preset and location flow. | Restaurant confirmation of specific facts and guest acceptance. |
| Configured service links can take guests to a provider. | Show the action and its unavailable state. The provider completes the action. | An authorized real-provider confirmation for the exact service and location. |
| The local tools support preview diffs and recovery snapshots. | Use a disposable copy and demonstrate the selected edit and recovery path. | A restaurant operator completing a routine update and recovery independently. |
| The kit includes nine illustrative restaurant presets. | Open the [preset design catalog](../preset-designs.md). | Permission and evidence for any named client or case study. |
| The guest pages are designed for readable text and clear controls. | Show menu text, headings, and links in the generated site. | Audited conformance for a specific build, with manual and assistive-technology evidence. |

Hold claims about bookings, orders, revenue, fewer phone calls, search ranking, speed, conformance, setup time, pricing, and integrations until the specific claim has evidence. Label sample numbers and examples as illustrative. Use exact scope in captions and disclosures. A provider click measures arrival at a handoff, while the provider must confirm a completed transaction.

## Adapt by channel

- **Homepage.** Name the category, give the guest benefit, and invite the reader to see a relevant example.
- **Social.** Use one task and one image or example. Put the meaningful copy in the post as well as the image. Caption screen recordings and use plain-text links where the channel allows.
- **Sales.** Open with the prospect’s current guest question. Show a guest path, a change the operator might make, and who would maintain it.
- **Help and status.** Use present tense, name the item or service, and offer the next available action. Guest-facing details belong to the restaurant’s own voice and factual review.
