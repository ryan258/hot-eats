# Hot Eats marketing and launch playbook

**Campaign line**

Make it easy to come in.

**Category**

A configurable restaurant website kit for independent restaurants and the web partners who build for them.

**Status**

Proposed pilot campaign and reusable copy. The copy assumes a demo and an approved reply route. Pricing, availability, licensing, hosting, and support terms still need an owner decision.

## Who to approach first

Start with an independent restaurant that already has an operator responsible for the menu and hours, an existing web partner or a willing maintainer, and an immediate reason to update its site. An outdated menu, a second location, a seasonal service change, or recurring questions about parking and hours gives the demo a concrete job.

| Reader | Situation to listen for | Show first | Ask for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owner or general manager | Guests call about facts the site ought to answer. A refresh or a new location is coming up. | The menu, location details, and the difference a schedule exception makes. | One guest question they answer most often. |
| Independent designer, developer, or small web studio | A restaurant project is in discovery and the team needs a starting point it can adapt. | Two preset designs, then the same guest task across both. Show how the maintainer reviews a menu or hours change. | One project and who will maintain it after launch. |
| Restaurant group with a few locations | Guests land on the wrong address, menu, or booking link. | The multi-location example and the path from selecting a location to the appropriate next step. | The location and service where routing breaks down today. |

Qualify a first pilot around one restaurant and one agreed guest journey. It needs an owner of the facts, a named technical maintainer, usable menus and access information, and approved contacts. Confirm who controls the domain and hosting and who owns each connected provider account. A request for a no-code editor, built-in checkout or reservations, or a managed hosting package requires a separate fit decision before any commitment.

## Offer and handoff

**First conversation**

Show a short walkthrough with illustrative data. Ask the prospect for their site link and one guest task. Close with a simple next step if there is a fit.

**Proposed pilot**

Agree on pages and locations, who supplies and approves facts, the enabled provider destinations and fallbacks, what constitutes acceptance, and who maintains the website after handoff. Build the restaurant's independent copy and review it privately. Start with a menu, hours and location, contact details, and one provider link or a direct contact. Add a module when there is a real guest task, reviewed content, and someone to keep it accurate.

**Possible commercial routes**

An assisted website build or a kit for technical partners. The first tests demand from restaurants. The second tests whether someone else can build and maintain an independent copy. Quote work only after deciding which route is on offer and recording who owns setup, hosting, content changes, source access, and support. Keep software licensing, media rights, and demo access explicit before distribution.

**Handoff to the maintainer**

Supply the named build and preview commands, approved data, the recovery path, and the agreed responsibility for deploying updates. Have the maintainer make and recover one representative change with a disposable copy. Review any production artifact, provider configuration, and release separately. Let guests search dishes, browse categories, see what’s sold out, and print the menu. Each dish can have a link of its own.

### Help guests find the right location

Show directions, hours and exceptions, and details like where to park or how to enter. Give each location its own page when you have more than one.

### Give guests a clear next step

Connect the ordering or reservation service you use where it’s set up. Give guests an approved contact option if a configured service is paused or unavailable.

### Start with a design that fits the restaurant

Explore layouts for a diner, café, bakery, food truck, and other formats. Choose the pages your restaurant needs and make the site your own.

### Keep everyday changes manageable

Preview changes to menus and hours before saving. A maintainer can use local tools to review what changed and recover a saved version.

## Five-minute demo script

Start the existing local preview with `./he preview --preset diner`. Open the example for the guest portion and the preset studio for the partner portion. Use a preset guide link to select the multi-location example. Keep these addresses for local demos or screen shares. [Local diner example](http://127.0.0.1:1417/) · [Preset studio](http://127.0.0.1:1417/site-kit/#presets)

1. **0:00–0:45 · Begin with the question.** “Say I’m on my phone and deciding where to eat. Can I check the menu and know where to go?” Say this is an illustrative restaurant.
2. **0:45–1:45 · Show one menu task.** Open the menu. Search a dish that appears in the example, then open its dish link or show its availability. Leave the feature list for later.
3. **1:45–2:45 · Show the visit.** Open location details, then find the schedule, address, directions, and one access fact. Use a location selector where the example has more than one location.
4. **2:45–3:30 · Explain the handoff.** Open the example’s ordering or reservation page where enabled. Show its unavailable state and any displayed fallback. “For a real restaurant, we review the provider link for this location and service. The provider takes the order or reservation.”
5. **3:30–4:30 · Show maintenance or design.** For an owner, run the read-only `./he today` or `./he menu`, then describe reviewing a change before saving. For a web partner, compare two designs in the guide and open a rendered component with its template call.
6. **4:30–5:00 · Close on their next step.** “Which of those questions would you want your site to answer first?” Write down their answer, who owns the facts, and who would maintain the site.

When demonstrating an edit, use a disposable client copy with a visible diff. Use a local or recorded demo when a shareable demo destination has yet to be approved. ## Common questions

**Can we use the ordering or reservations service we have?**

Hot Eats can send guests to a reviewed provider URL for the right location and service. The provider handles the order, reservation, and confirmation. We would review your actual destination and the fallback contact before release.

**Who can update the menu and hours?**

Hot Eats has local commands for everyday changes and CSV tools for editing existing dishes. Your project needs a named maintainer who is comfortable with the workflow and can publish the updated site.

**Can we use our own look?**

Yes. A preset is a starting point for composition and component layouts. The independent restaurant copy uses its own approved identity, content, and images.

**What does the kit run on?**

The local workflow uses Hugo Extended 0.166.x and Python 3.11 or later. It builds a static website. Confirm the host and update workflow as part of the project scope.

**Does it support multiple locations?**

Hot Eats includes per-location pages, schedules, visit details, and configured provider destinations. Use the multi-location example to check the route you need before scoping a real site.

**How do we review accessibility?**

We can show text menus, location-specific access information, and the guest tasks that need review. A real site and any linked provider need their own device and assistive-technology checks.

**What does it cost and what support is included?**

A pilot proposal would spell out the build, content, hosting responsibilities, and ongoing updates after we agree the scope.

## Channel plan and measurement

| Channel | Initial unit of work | Measure | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm owner introductions | A personal email and a five-minute demo. | Qualified conversations per introduction and repeated guest tasks. | Lead with the task owners recognize without prompting. |
| Web partner outreach | One paired preset example and the maintenance workflow. | Partners with an active fit, named maintenance ownership, and an agreed next step. | Continue when the kit fits the partner’s handoff model. |
| Owned site and social | One focused landing page and a reusable labeled screen recording. | Inquiries that identify a restaurant and a concrete problem. Ask how they found Hot Eats. | Clarify the promise or action if people view the demo but replies suggest a different product. |
| Search content | One page or article answering a real restaurant-site question, such as publishing a searchable menu or keeping holiday hours clear. | Qualified inquiries tied to the page when attribution is available. | Write the next page from repeated buyer questions. |
| Paid search | Consider one narrowly defined test after the offer and path are proven in conversations, with an approved spend and a stop rule. | Cost per qualified inquiry and recurring reasons for poor fit. | Stop at the agreed cap or when inquiry quality falls below the agreed threshold. |

For the first round, **five conversations and one clearly scoped pilot** are planning targets. Track `date | segment | source | guest task | maintainer | stage | next step` in a private note. Count each prospect once. A qualified conversation has a real restaurant or project, a guest task the kit can serve, and a plausible maintainer. Count an accepted pilot when scope, ownership, and acceptance tasks are agreed.

For pilot evaluation, record which tasks guests could finish and where they needed help. Ask the maintainer to review, save, and recover a representative edit. If the restaurant wants to compare recurring call topics, use the same question and observation window before and after the change. Provider-reported completions need their own period, definition, and permission. Report sample size, relevant dates, and limitations before using results publicly. Start with manual notes and approved provider reports.
